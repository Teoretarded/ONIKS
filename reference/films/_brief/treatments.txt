TREATMENTS: six films, three per style. Timings are guides; the story order, the one-take rule and the signature are not.
Each builder: read your block, and skim the others so yours stays distinct.

OA  oa_scale.html   ORBITAL A: SCALE                  (~165 s)   sources: o3_orbit, o1_ignition, o2_seaskim, o4_chronograph
=====================================================================================================
Signature: POWERS OF TEN. One unbroken take from 900 km up down to a metre from the missile's skin, and back up. The
subject is scale and machinery: a globe, a map, a coastline, a battery, a launch, a flight over the sea. No engagement,
no target, no strike: the round simply flies away over the horizon while the camera climbs home to orbit.
 1 Orbit (0-15): wireframe Earth limb, graticule, terminator (o3). A satellite (HD.satellite) glides past close to the
   lens, solar wings and mesh reflector in full hairline detail, its ground track sweeping over the theatre.
 2 Theatre (15-35): the o3 dive onto the real seed-1337 map: coast, depth contours, place names, the battery at
   Krasnaya Kosa marked in yellow. Altitude/scale readout (log ruler).
 3 Battery (35-55): the map's contour lines become the bluff and the sea swell of o1 (match-move, no cut). Low fly-in
   past the Monolith-B (HD.radar, array turning), the Pantsir (HD.pantsir, search radar spinning) to TEL 1: jacks down,
   erecting on its hydraulic ram. Clock T-.
 4 Launch (55-78): o1 cold launch (gas ejection, cap tumbling, ignition, pitch-over); the camera cranes up and swings
   alongside; booster ejected out of the nozzle (HD.oniksBooster tumbling away), wings and fins unfold, ramjet lights
   with shock diamonds. Macro details: intake lip, wing hinge, nozzle.
 5 Over the sea (78-110): o2 chase at low level over the wire sea: Mach cone, V-shock on the water, spray; a pair of
   F/A-18Es (HD.fighter, fan faces and nozzles visible) crosses high overhead the other way; the camera slows and lets
   the round pull away toward the horizon until it is a point.
 6 Climb (110-165): the camera rises straight up through the layers: the sea, the coast, the map returns with the
   round's track as one yellow line off toward the horizon, the theatre square, the globe, back to orbit where the
   satellite comes round again = frame 0.
Layout: o1/o3-like: menu column left, readouts top right, Fig. caption bottom right.

OB  ob_long_exposure.html   ORBITAL B: LONG EXPOSURE   (~160 s)   sources: o4_chronograph, o1_ignition, o2_seaskim
=====================================================================================================
Signature: TIME MADE VISIBLE. A Marey chronophotograph that is also a film: a long-lens, near-orthographic camera
running side-on on a virtual rail; every 0.35 s (simulated time) a hairline ghost of every moving thing is stamped
onto the plate and stays. The plate grows into one enormous figure of motion, then rewinds. It is about motion itself:
no engagement, no target; the rounds simply fly out of the plate over the horizon.
 1 Cap (0-15): extreme macro on a TLC end cap (bolt ring, seal, hairline detail); the lens pulls back through the
   launcher to reveal the battery side-on: three TELs (HD.tel) erecting on their rams at staggered phases, the rams'
   telescoping stages stamped as they extend.
 2 Ripple (15-42): four rounds leave 2.5 s apart (TEL 1 R/L, TEL 2 R/L). Each ejection, tumbling cap, gas puff,
   ignition, pitch-over and booster ejection is stamped: overlapping strobe arcs. A chronograph clock with hundredths.
 3 Cruise (42-85): the rail camera accelerates and paces the four rounds in line abreast low over the sea; exposures
   string out as pearls; wings unfolding caught frame by frame; the compressed-range ruler (honestly labelled, as in
   o4) ticks km; time-warp shown. A patrolling MH-60R (HD.helo) crosses the lens low over the water and its rotor is
   stamped too: a disc of ghost blades. Later a pair of F/A-18Es (HD.fighter) high above, fan faces and nozzles strobed.
 4 Horizon (85-108): the rounds shrink over the curve of the sea; the last exposures are points; the rail slows.
 5 The plate (108-130): the lens pulls back to show the entire figure at once, labelled like a scientific plate
   ("Fig. 4 · 4 rounds · N exposures · 0.35 s"), rulers, time marks, one yellow highlighter on TEL 1's first ejection.
 6 Rewind (130-160): the plate un-stamps in reverse while the rail runs back to the battery at speed (clock running
   backwards, "-x40"), rounds fly back into their tubes, caps return, TELs lower... ending on the macro cap = frame 0.
Layout: o4-like: big "Oniks" wordmark top-left, menu as a row along the bottom.

OC  oc_underway.html   ORBITAL C: UNDERWAY           (~170 s)   sources: o5_terminal, o2_seaskim, o3_orbit
=====================================================================================================
Signature: THE SHIP AS A MACHINE. A night at sea with the strike group, in full hairline detail: radar arcs (SPY-1
search pulses as expanding shell arcs, as in o5), the SPS radar turning, heat shimmer off the stacks, flight
operations. Peaceful, vast, mechanical. No engagement.
 1 Watch (0-24): night, a slow dolly along HD.destroyer underway from bow to stern: Kelvin wake, bow wave, SPY pulses
   sweeping out as shell arcs, SPS turning, heat shimmer (wavy hairlines) from the stacks, Phalanx mounts at rest,
   lifelines, boats, the 5" gun. On the flight deck the MH-60R (HD.helo) starts: blades droop, then spin up.
 2 Lift (24-50): the helo lifts off; the camera rises with it off the stern, the destroyer shrinking below; the helo
   banks out over the dark sea, its rotor disc and tail rotor in close-up.
 3 The group (50-95): the camera flies with the helo along the formation: a cruiser, then the carrier (HD.carrier):
   deck lines in hairline, a pair of F/A-18Es (HD.fighter) on the catapults; one launches (catapult stroke,
   afterburner, fan faces visible as it passes the lens) and climbs away; the second follows.
 4 High (95-125): the camera climbs with the fighters to altitude: the whole group below as small wire shapes with
   their wakes, radar arcs pulsing from each ship, the coastline far off; the fighters peel away.
 5 Return (125-150): descending again, the camera meets the helo on its way back and follows it to the destroyer's
   flight deck: approach, flare, touchdown, blades slowing.
 6 Next ship (150-170): the camera lifts off the deck and glides across the formation to another destroyer of the
   group, arriving in exactly the opening dolly = frame 0.
Layout: o5-like: menu column left, a small quiet log right, Fig. caption bottom right.

PA  pa_picture.html   POINT CLOUD A: THE PICTURE     (~165 s)   sources: p2_clutter, p5_confidence, p3_coastline, p1_strike, p4_track
=====================================================================================================
Signature: THE RADAR PICTURE. The film is about building belief from returns: the whole sensor picture around the
coast as spectacle. It starts and ends on the Monolith-B 3D clutter scope; everything in between is the camera flying
inside the sensors' own data. The most "radar scans" film. No engagement.
 1 Search (0-22): p2 scope around the site: sea-clutter height field, the rotating beam with its lime edge, false
   tracks dropped, TRK 21 firming up hit by hit ("? 0.31 -> DDG? 0.64"). HD.radar array turning at the centre.
 2 Ride the beam (22-45): the camera lifts off and rides the beam outward along TRK 21's bearing, clutter streaming
   under it, range rings rolling past, a true range readout climbing; clutter thins with range.
 3 Confidence (45-80): p5 at the track: an uncertainty cloud pulled onto a hull by each pulse (HD.destroyer sampled),
   class bars Unknown -> Frigate? -> DDG · Arleigh Burke 0.89; a second contact forms nearby into a different class;
   a rain squall passes as a patch of clutter that never becomes a track.
 4 Coast (80-98): the camera turns back along the radar horizon toward land; p3's LiDAR scan line materialises the
   real seed-1337 coastline as it passes over.
 5 Strike (98-118): at the battery, p1's lightning-strike scan reveals TEL (HD.tel), radar and Pantsir (HD.pantsir)
   with part boxes (ram, caps, wheels, TLCs, guns, radars).
 6 Track (118-140): the EO tracker on the bluff (p4 reticle, lag, boxes on true bounds, magnified inset) follows an
   MH-60R (HD.helo) crossing the coast: boxes on rotor head, tail rotor, fuselage; the inset shows the blades.
 7 Picture (140-165): the camera climbs back into the scope; the helicopter is now a track on it; the sweep goes on and
   a new contact begins to form exactly as at frame 0.
Layout: p1/p2-like: menu column left, kick label top-left, one stat top-right.

PB  pb_survey.html   POINT CLOUD B: SURVEY          (~165 s)   sources: p3_coastline, p2_clutter, p5_confidence, p4_track, p1_strike
=====================================================================================================
Signature: THE DRONE'S EYE. A chase camera rides behind an Orlan-10-class survey drone (HD.drone; its prop disc in
the foreground, control surfaces, the EO ball turning); everything is revealed by the drone's own sensors: LiDAR over
land, a radar swath over the sea, the EO tracker box. Dawn: a faint lime-grey horizon band on graphite. A survey
flight, start to finish; no engagement.
 1 Catapult (0-18): at the battery, the drone on its pneumatic rail (HD.catapult), prop spinning up (blades as a dot
   disc), launch: the camera rides the carriage then lifts away with the drone; a mini lightning-strike scan (p1)
   tags the battery below as it passes.
 2 Coastline (18-58): p3's LiDAR survey along the real coast: scan line builds cliffs, ravines, the waterline; tags the
   radar, the Pantsir, the battery, landmarks; the 360° ping reveals the whole coast as the drone turns seaward.
 3 Over water (58-88): its radar paints a moving swath of sea clutter below (p2 texture seen from above): wind rows,
   sea spikes, a rain squall, false alarms rejected; a contact emerges at the edge of the swath.
 4 Contact (88-118): a wide standoff orbit; the contact forms from an uncertainty cloud into HD.destroyer (p5 class
   bars); the EO ball turns to it: p4's tracking box and magnified inset show its detail (radar turning, helo on
   deck, wake).
 5 Squall (118-138): the drone turns home through a rain squall: the radar and LiDAR returns fill with rain speckle,
   the picture degrades and recovers, the coast reappears ahead.
 6 Home (138-165): over the surveyed terrain, the drone deploys its recovery parachute near the battery, airbag,
   settles; the camera drifts to the catapult where the next drone waits, prop starting = frame 0.
Layout: p3-like: menu column right, the chase composed left-of-centre.

PC  pc_anatomy.html   POINT CLOUD C: ANATOMY       (~160 s)   sources: p1_strike, p4_track, p3_coastline, p2_clutter, p5_confidence
=====================================================================================================
Signature: X-RAY. LiDAR that sees through things: the scan passes through steel and shows what is inside, like a
museum cutaway in dots. The film is the life of one round from its container to over the horizon, and the reload that
puts the next one in the tube. No engagement, no target, no guidance or countermeasure logic: it is about the machine
and the battery, shown in the most beautiful dots of the six films.
 1 Inside (0-20): close on a TEL at night; p1's lightning-strike scan passes THROUGH the TLC walls: the dots reveal the
   Oniks inside (HD.oniks: folded wings, booster, intake cover) and its tags condense as the evidence builds (p5-style
   confidence: "OBJ in TLC 2 · 3M55 · 0.97").
 2 Exploded view (20-45): the round's parts float apart along its axis in a slow museum cutaway (intake + radome,
   body sections, ramjet chamber, booster, wings, fins), each tagged with its public designation and size, then glide
   back together. Keep it at the level of a museum placard or a public reference book.
 3 Erect (45-62): back out through the container wall; the TEL deploys: jacks down, the hydraulic ram extends (tagged,
   dots flowing along the telescoping stages), the launcher rises to vertical, the cap line flickers.
 4 Launch (62-82): p4's EO tracker takes over: reticle, lag, boxes on the cap, gas cloud, plume and the round on its true
   bounds, magnified inset; booster ejected from the nozzle, wings unfold (all visible in the inset).
 5 Coast (82-112): the camera follows the round's plume out over the edge of the bluff; p3's scan line materialises the
   real seed-1337 coastline beneath as it passes; the round shrinks toward the horizon over p2's sea texture seen from
   above; its plume dots drift and fade.
 6 Reload (112-160): the camera sinks back to the battery; a transloader crane truck (build it from GEO primitives in
   your page, or stub from HD.tel's chassis) swings a fresh TLC onto the TEL; the X-ray scan passes through it and the
   next round appears inside = frame 0.
Layout: p4/p5-like: menu as a centred row along the bottom, compact readout top-left.
