# Visual reference sheet (look-dev only)

Legend: [V] = URL returned by a search this session (page contents not all opened). [S] = known source, find by title (URL not verified). No video timestamps were recorded (videos not opened); scrub for the named beat. Numbers marked ~ are rough or commonly cited; check against footage.

## 1. Ship hull break-up, fires, sinking
Sources
- [V] https://www.twz.com/42129/watch-the-ex-uss-ingraham-frigate-get-its-back-broken-by-a-torpedo : Navy video, ex-USS Ingraham SINKEX (2021). Torpedo breaks the back; hull splits, both halves settle.
- [V] https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/Article/2736525/us-forces-conduct-sinking-exercise/ : Navy SINKEX release.
- [V] https://www.navytimes.com/news/your-military/2026/07/30/see-what-its-like-to-be-on-a-ship-getting-hit-by-us-missiles/ : 2026 Peleliu / Mobile Bay SINKEX, b-roll from inside the hull.
- [S] DVIDS (dvidshub.net), search "SINKEX": RIMPAC sinkings (ex-Tarawa, ex-Durham, ex-Dubuque...), public domain.
- [S] "Sinking of ex-USS America (CV-66)" Navy release: carrier burning for days, long oil/smoke footage.
- [S] Navy / Norwegian / Swedish SINKEX with Harpoon or NSM hits.
Look
- Missile hit: short white-yellow flash, orange fireball ~1-3 s, grey-white vapor puff, then dark brown/black smoke column. Dirty fat column, not a clean mushroom.
- Underkeel torpedo: white water column fed by the bubble pulse; hull lifts amidships, hogs, keel breaks, bow and stern drop and tilt into a "V" over ~10-30 s.
- Sinking: ~1-2 min after a break; burning hulls last hours to days. Bow rises steeply as the stern goes.
- Smoke: black-brown, billowing, wind-tilted, flat base on the sea. Oil fire on water: orange sheets and tongues, low flame height, huge soot column.
- Debris: slow arcs of light panels and white spray; few large pieces. Water goes foamy white, then dark oil slick.

## 2. Fireballs, debris, shock rings
Sources
- [S] Declassified nuclear test films (LLNL / archive.org, public domain): Crossroads Baker, Castle, Upshot-Knothole. Water column, base surge, condensation cloud, colour sequence.
- [S] Navy shock trials (USS Winston S. Churchill 2001, USS Mesa Verde 2008), DVIDS: underwater explosion plume and hull whip.
- [S] Beirut 2020 port blast footage (many angles): condensation ring crossing the frame, orange-red NO2 cloud.
- [S] Antares OA-5 failure 2014 and Saturn V launch footage (NASA TV): fireball plus sparkler debris trails.
Look
- Colour: white-yellow flash (a few frames) -> yellow-orange -> orange-red -> dark red -> black soot. Core stays brighter, rim cools first.
- Expansion: fast in first 2-5 frames, then decelerates; outer shock ring (faint ripple, white condensation ring ~0.5-2 s) travels ahead of the fireball.
- Rise: after ~1-3 s buoyancy forms a stem and toroid (vortex ring), rising ~20-50 m/s; mushroom cap in ~10-30 s.
- Debris: dark chunks on ballistic arcs; sparks are thin streaks with slight curve; white-hot metal fades yellow -> orange -> red -> gone in ~0.3-1.5 s.

## 3. Hypervelocity impacts in space
Sources
- [V] https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/avgr.pdf : NASA Ames Vertical Gun Range fact sheet (to ~7 km/s, Phantom and Shimadzu cameras).
- [V] https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180007384/downloads/20180007384.pdf : AVGR imaging upgrade.
- [V] https://repository.library.brown.edu/studio/item/bdr:11282 : Brown dissertation, ejecta velocity vs time from AVGR high-speed video.
- [S] NASA Orbital Debris Program Office (orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov): hypervelocity impact lab, Whipple shield shots, DebriSat break-up photos.
- [S] ESA / Fraunhofer EMI hypervelocity shield tests; ESA Space Debris Office animations.
- [S] NASA LCROSS (2009) and Deep Impact (2005) impact frames.
Look
- Flash: intense white-blue in microseconds, then orange-red glowing vapor fading in ms to seconds.
- Ejecta: expanding inverted cone (~45 deg wall) with bright central jet; fine particles lead, coarse follow.
- In vacuum, fragments fly straight at constant speed (no drag); spin shows as glints; fragments are dark once cold.
- Thin plates: clean entry hole, cone of spray behind; Whipple bumpers vaporise; panels curl into petals around the hole.
- Test cameras: tens of thousands fps; whole event is a few ms.

## 4. Re-entry and hypersonic plasma
Sources
- [V] https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2020/12/Reentry_of_Hayabusa2_s_sample_capsule : Hayabusa2 fireball (visible 53 s, 12 km/s).
- [V] https://www.hayabusa2.jaxa.jp/en/topics/20190822e_Capsule/ : JAXA capsule return plan.
- [V] https://space.com/spacex-starship-third-test-flight-reentry-video : Starship flight 3 onboard re-entry video.
- [V] https://aquarid.physics.uwo.ca/research/fireball/events/Chel/overview.html : Western Univ. Chelyabinsk fireball.
- [V] https://www.popsci.com/article/science/what-we-now-know-about-chelyabinsk-meteor/ : summary (19 m, ~19 km/s, ~500 kt, peak ~19 miles up).
- [S] NASA footage: Shuttle re-entry seen from ISS, Stardust 2006 airborne imaging, Orion EFT-1 / Artemis I.
Look
- Glow onset ~90-100 km, strong by 70-80 km; capsule peak heating ~55-70 km.
- Colours: orange-yellow ablator streaks (sodium), pink-violet-magenta shock layer from N2/O, whiter-blue at higher speed. Starship: orange-white wash windward, violet fringe, orange flecks from lost tiles.
- Shape: bright bow-shock rim ahead of a blunt nose, long comet tail of ablation debris; Hayabusa2 a bright golden head that fragments into a streak for ~tens of km.
- Chelyabinsk: sudden glare brighter than the Sun, thick white trail twisting for minutes, boom arrives ~90 s later.

## 5. Railgun firing
Sources
- [V] https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Media/Images/igphoto/2002572121/ : NAVSEA high-speed frame, record EMRG shot.
- [V] https://www.popsci.com/military-aviation-space/article/2008-02/navy-tests-32-megajoule-railgun : Jan 2008 shot, 10.64 MJ, 2,520 m/s.
- [V] https://csmonitor.com/Science/2012/0301/Navy-railgun-fires-40-lb.-bullets-at-Mach-7 : BAE prototype tests at Dahlgren.
- [V] https://www.eurekalert.org/multimedia/544701 : EMRG image.
- [S] YouTube: ONR / NSWC Dahlgren releases (2008, 2010 33 MJ, 2012 BAE); GA-EMS Blitzer; Japan ATLA railgun test footage.
Look
- Muzzle: very bright white core, orange-yellow tongue and a fat plume of burning armature/projectile fragments (no propellant gas), sparks, then light smoke.
- Slug ~Mach 6-7: bright dot with hot comet trail; sabot petals split in ms and tumble as dim plates with glowing edges.
- Timing: bore transit a few ms; flash tens of ms; visible plume ~1 s. Slow-mo is ~10k-100k fps. Breech end stays dark, no blowback.

## 6. Fast object entering water
Sources
- [V] https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.01540 (and ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1704.01540) : Water entry of deformable spheres, high-speed sequences (cavities, pinch-off).
- [V] https://cpb.iphy.ac.cn/article/doi/10.1088/1674-1056/27/9/094703 : hot spheres into water, nucleate vs film boiling cavities.
- [V] https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/etd/7243 : Utah State dissertation on impact cavities.
- [V] https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/50584 : MIT thesis, subsurface cavities / supercavitation.
- [S] Edgerton Digital Collections (edgerton-digital-collections.org): bullet and splash photographs.
- [S] Supercavitation test films on YouTube (search "supercavitation test footage").
Look
- Fast entry: thin splash crown rising and closing; open air cavity behind the body with slightly rippled walls; pinch-off at roughly 0.5-1 cavity depth sends a jet up and down.
- Supercavity: long glassy bore, silvery wall, ragged bubbly closure, trailing bubble cloud.
- Hot body: white steam sheath; film boiling gives a smooth cavity with a vapor gap; hotter/faster gives broken ragged cavities.
- Afterwards: dome of white foam and mist, rings of surface waves, milky bubble plume rising for seconds, steam wisps; collapse neck then a tall central spray jet.

## 7. Cryogenics and pulsed power
Sources
- [V] https://www.sandia.gov/z-machine/about-z/how-z-works/ : Z machine, 36 Marx banks, 60 x 2.6 uF capacitors each, 85 kV charge, ~20 MJ.
- [V] https://sandia.gov/z-machine/?p=84 : Marx bank page.
- [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marx_generator : layout and diagram.
- [V] https://ga.com/sandia-awards-gaep-entire-zr-upgrade-marx-capacitor-production : capacitor modules.
- [S] LLNL NIF photo library (lasers.llnl.gov): capacitor / power conditioning bays.
- [S] CERN and Fermilab image libraries: vacuum-jacketed transfer lines, cryoplants. Wikimedia Commons: "Vacuum insulated pipe", "Cryocooler", "Dewar".
- [S] ONR / NSWC Dahlgren and GA-EMS pulsed-power containers.
Look
- Capacitor banks: rows of rectangular cans in racks, terminals on top, wide flat copper bus plates, thick HV cable bundles, white ceramic insulators; Marx banks often oil-immersed with a row of switches.
- Cryolines: shiny stainless vacuum-jacketed pipes with bellows and flanges every few metres, white frost only at valves, ends and leaks.
- Vented LN2 gives a dense ground-hugging white fog that falls and rolls (not a rising plume); gaseous helium is nearly invisible.
- Cold heads: finned cylinders with a motor on top and flex hoses to a compressor; frost thickest at the cold end.
