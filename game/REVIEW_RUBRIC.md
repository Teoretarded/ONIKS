# Review rubric

A reviewer plays the game in the browser and scores it **1-10**:

- **1-4** not good: broken, unplayable, or does not look like the films.
- **4-7** not bad, but it could clearly be more interesting, have more in it, look closer to the films.
- **7-8** passes. Anything listed as an improvement must say whether it is worth the time and tokens.
- **9-10** would impress the user.

What the user asked for (score against these, in this order):

1. **Looks exactly like the reference films** (`reference/films`, Point Cloud style first): graphite, white dots,
   lime scans, coral hostile; the realistic, detailed models; how everything moves. Nothing that looks like a
   website template, no bright panels, no taglines.
2. **It is a real RTS**: select, order, fight, win or lose; the AI puts up a fight; the time rate is shown.
3. **Full 360° camera**, from close to the models to far above the map.
4. **The favourite effects as gameplay**: the lime lightning SCAN that identifies parts, X-ray, exploded views
   floating apart and back, reloads, intercepts and hits, radar, the sea. Lightning is a feature.
5. **Anatomy**: the Inspect view is the showpiece.
6. **Maps are unique**; three modes work: Sandbox, Combat, Campaign (films as the story).
7. **Realism**: real sizes, speeds, timings, true labels, boxes that fit.
8. **Performance**: smooth (< 16 ms/frame) at 1920x1080; no console errors.
9. **Polish**: nothing broken, flows between screens, feel.

The review lists: the score, what is best, what is weakest, the top 10 concrete fixes ranked by impact
(each with where in the code, and whether it can be given to a separate agent), and anything broken.
