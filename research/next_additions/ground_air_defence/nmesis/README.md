# NMESIS (Navy/Marine Expeditionary Ship Interdiction System)

Role: US Marine Corps land-based anti-ship missile launcher — pairs the Naval Strike Missile already in the
roster with an unmanned launch vehicle, giving the carrier-side player a land-based sea-denial option to mirror
the coastal side's truck-mounted Bastion/Bal launchers.

## Specs

- Launch vehicle: ROGUE-Fires, an unmanned variant of the Oshkosh JLTV chassis — [Naval News](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2021/04/here-is-our-first-look-at-the-usmcs-nmesis-nsm-being-launched-from-an-unmanned-jltv/)
- Missile load: up to 2 Naval Strike Missiles per launcher (this folder's NSM specs cover the round itself) —
  [Army Recognition](https://www.armyrecognition.com/military-products/navy/weapons-systems/missiles/nmesis-navy-marine-expeditionary-ship-interdiction-system)
- Crew: none on the launcher itself — teleoperated remotely, keeping Marines away from the firing point —
  [Military Aerospace](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/sensors/article/14303476/raytheon-technologies-corp-anti-ship-missiles-shore-defense)
- First live fire by Marines: Fox Battery, 2nd Bn., 11th Marines, NAS Point Mugu, 27 June 2023 —
  [DVIDS image caption](https://www.dvidshub.net/image/7918237/2nd-bn-11th-marines-becomes-first-marine-unit-fire-nmesis-missiles)
- Transportable by C-17 for rapid island/beach deployment — [DVIDS image caption](https://www.dvidshub.net/image/8188805/60th-aps-assists-new-equipment-training-team)

## Visible parts / what it looks like in action

- A stripped-down JLTV hull with no crew cab — just a chassis, sensor mast and a canister launch box on the bed,
  giving it a noticeably lower, more skeletal silhouette than a crewed truck launcher.
- Vertical-style launch cells hold the missiles at a raised angle on the vehicle bed, cued by camera/sensor mast
  rather than a driver's cab.
- Movement: teleoperated, so it can be seen driving into a firing position and repositioning afterward with no
  visible occupants — a "ghost truck" behaviour distinct from crewed TELs.
- Loading/deployment: shown being winched or driven onto a C-17 for airlift — the system's selling point is rapid
  movement between islands/beaches rather than a fixed coastal battery.
