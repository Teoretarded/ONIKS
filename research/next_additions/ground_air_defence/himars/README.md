# M142 HIMARS

Role: US wheeled, truck-mounted rocket artillery — a land-attack round the carrier-group/Marine side lacks
against coastal targets, mirroring the coastal side's own truck-launched systems (Tor-M2, NMESIS-style trucks).
Fires GMLRS-family rockets or a single ATACMS/PrSM long-range missile from the same launcher.

## Specs

- Length: 6.94 m — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M142_HIMARS)
- Combat weight: ~16.25 t — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M142_HIMARS)
- Crew: 3 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M142_HIMARS)
- Max road speed: 94 km/h; road range ~480 km — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M142_HIMARS)
- Armament: 1x 6-round M270-family rocket pod (GMLRS, ~70-150 km depending on variant) or 1x MGM-140 ATACMS/PrSM
  missile — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M142_HIMARS)
- Chassis: FMTV 5-ton truck base — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M142_HIMARS)

## Visible parts / what it looks like in action

- A single rectangular launch pod (6 rocket tubes, or one large ATACMS/PrSM canister) mounted on a turntable atop
  a standard 6-wheel FMTV truck bed — much lighter and smaller than a tracked TEL like Bastion-P.
- Pod elevates to a steep firing angle before launch; the truck cab is armoured but unremarkable, so the raised
  pod angle is the clearest "about to fire" tell.
- Launch signature: a short, very bright flash and dense white/grey smoke cloud from each tube in rapid
  succession (for GMLRS) or one large single launch (for ATACMS/PrSM) — visually louder and smokier than a single
  vertical-cell missile launch.
- "Shoot and scoot": the truck displaces immediately after firing, a fast road-speed relocation rather than
  staying in position like a fixed coastal battery.
