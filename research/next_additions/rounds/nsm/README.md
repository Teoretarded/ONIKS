# Naval Strike Missile (NSM)

Role: Kongsberg/Raytheon low-signature, sea-skimming anti-ship missile. Fielded on US Littoral Combat Ships,
Constellation-class frigates, Arleigh Burke destroyers (from 2024) and the NMESIS truck launcher — the same round
arms ship, air and land platforms.

## Specs

- Length: 3.95 m — [Kongsberg product page](https://www.kongsberg.com/globalassets/kongsberg-defence-and-aerospace/kda_brochures/nsm_brochure.pdf)
- Weight: ~410-420 kg (booster + missile) — [Kongsberg product page](https://www.kongsberg.com/globalassets/kongsberg-defence-and-aerospace/kda_brochures/nsm_brochure.pdf)
- Speed: high subsonic — [US Navy, NAVSEA fact file](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/)
- Range: over 100 nautical miles (~185+ km) — [US Navy, NSM procurement release](https://www.navy.mil/)
- Warhead: penetrating blast-fragmentation, ~120 kg class — [Kongsberg product page](https://www.kongsberg.com/globalassets/kongsberg-defence-and-aerospace/kda_brochures/nsm_brochure.pdf)
- First US Navy shipboard firing: USS *Coronado* (LCS-4), September 2014 —
  [Naval Today](https://www.navaltoday.com/2014/09/24/uss-coronado-fires-naval-strike-missile/)
- First US destroyer firing: USS *Fitzgerald* (DDG-62), RIMPAC, 18 July 2024 —
  [US Navy release](https://www.dvidshub.net/)

## Visible parts / what it looks like in action

- Stored and fired from a slanted deck-mounted canister box launcher (distinct from a vertical-launch cell) —
  on LCS a pair of angled box launchers amidships, on Burke-class a similar canister rack added topside.
  strikes a visually different launch profile from the vertical Tomahawk/SM-6 cells already modelled.
  It is a low-signature, composite-bodied missile — no visible rocket motor casing, small pop-out wings and tail
  fins after the booster stage falls away.
- Launch sequence: booster ignites inside the canister with a short, sharp flash and smoke burst, missile exits
  at a shallow upward angle, then noses over to a very low sea-skimming cruise altitude.
- In cruise the missile flies close to the wave tops with a thin white exhaust trail, small enough in profile to
  read as a fast, low silhouette rather than a large climbing plume.
