# Steregushchiy (Project 20380)

Role: Russia's newest coastal corvette class — a small, stealthy multirole hull for ASW and point air defence
close to shore, filling the "Med" gap below the Gorshkov/Grigorovich frigates and above the Buyan-M/Karakurt
corvettes already in the roster.

## Specs

- Length: 104.5 m; beam 13 m; draft 3.7 m — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steregushchiy-class_corvette)
- Displacement: 1,800 t standard, 2,200 t full — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steregushchiy-class_corvette)
- Speed: 27 kn; range 3,800 nmi at 14 kn; endurance 15 days — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steregushchiy-class_corvette)
- Crew: 90 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steregushchiy-class_corvette)
- Lead ship commissioned 28 February 2008 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steregushchiy-class_corvette)

## Shape and layout

Bow to stern: a low, sharply-raked stealth-faceted bow, a forward 100 mm A-190 gun mount, then flush anti-ship
launcher boxes (angled Kh-35, 2x4) forward of the low pyramidal bridge/mast superstructure carrying the flat-panel
Furke/Zaslon radar faces, a twin-arm AK-630M/Palash CIWS mount amidships (seen close-up in one of the user's
photos), a short helicopter hangar, and a full-width flight deck with landing pad aft for one Ka-27 —
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steregushchiy-class_corvette), [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/steregushchy-class/).

## Moving parts

- Main gun mount trains and elevates; CIWS twin barrels slew and elevate independently of the ship's heading.
- Kh-35 launcher box lids open before firing.
- Hangar door slides open to strike the Ka-27 below deck; helicopter blades fold for hangar stowage.

## What it looks like in action

A small, low-profile grey hull with sharply angled surfaces everywhere (hull, deckhouse, mast) rather than round
fittings — the class was an early Russian stealth-shaping design. At speed it throws a short, choppy wake typical
of a corvette-sized hull rather than a frigate's long bow wave.
