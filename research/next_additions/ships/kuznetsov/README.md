# Admiral Kuznetsov (Project 1143.5)

Role: Russia's sole aircraft carrier — STOBAR (ski-jump launch, arrested recovery) air power projection. Already
flagged in the top table as "Low" map fit (single unit, huge, scale mismatch for a 170x140 km coastal map) but
catalogued here since the user supplied reference photos.

## Specs

- Length: 305 m overall, 270 m waterline — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov)
- Beam: 72 m overall, 35 m waterline; draft 10 m — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov)
- Displacement: 58,600 t full load — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov)
- Speed: 29 kn; range 8,500 nmi at 18 kn — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov)
- Crew: 1,690 plus 626 air group and 40 flag staff — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov)
- Commissioned 1991; air wing up to ~26 fixed-wing (Su-33/MiG-29K) plus Ka-27/Ka-31 helicopters — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_carrier_Admiral_Kuznetsov)

## Shape and layout

Bow to stern: a 14° ski-jump ramp at the bow angling the forward flight deck upward, then the angled landing
strip running down the port side with 4 arrestor wires, a starboard-side island superstructure amidships bristling
with radar/sensor masts and the single funnel, deck-edge lifts forward and aft of the island moving aircraft to
the hangar deck below, and a stern loaded with defensive gun/missile systems rather than an angled catwalk like
US carriers — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuznetsov-class_aircraft_carrier).

## Moving parts

- Ski-jump ramp is fixed (no fold), but the arrestor cables and blast deflectors behind each takeoff spot retract
  into the deck.
- Deck-edge aircraft lifts (2) raise/lower between hangar and flight deck.
- No catapults — aircraft use full afterburner up the ramp instead.

## What it looks like in action

Aircraft queue at the ski-jump, light full afterburner, and rotate off the upward ramp at low airspeed rather than
being flung off a catapult track; on recovery they catch one of the angled deck's arrestor wires. The ship is
frequently shadowed by tugs and escorts, and its funnel trails heavy dark smoke from the steam plant, a
recognisable silhouette cue at range.
