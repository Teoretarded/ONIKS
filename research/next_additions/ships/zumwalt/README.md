# Zumwalt (DDG-1000)

Role: US Navy's stealth destroyer — a land-attack/gun-forward design with a heavily reduced radar signature,
sized between the Constellation frigate and a Burke-class destroyer in the current roster.

## Specs

- Length: 190 m (610 ft); beam 24.6 m; draft 8.4 m — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer)
- Displacement: ~16,000 t full load — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer)
- Speed: 30 kn — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer)
- Crew: 147, plus 28 in the air detachment — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer)
- USS *Zumwalt* commissioned 15 October 2016 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer)

## Shape and layout

Bow to stern: a sharply raked, knife-edge wave-piercing bow with no flare (the hull leans inward above the
waterline — "tumblehome"), two 155 mm Advanced Gun System turrets recessed into the foredeck, then the tall
pyramidal composite deckhouse (no external masts — antennas are embedded in its faceted panels) housing the
bridge and SPY-3 radar arrays, 80 Mk 57 VLS cells spread in peripheral modules along both sides of the hull rather
than one central pit, twin 57 mm gun mounts, a hangar, and a flight deck for two MH-60R — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer).

## Moving parts

- Both 155 mm AGS turrets train and elevate (the gun system itself is not operational fleet-wide as of the
  sources checked).
- Peripheral VLS cell hatches flip open before a missile launches out of the deck edge rather than a central deck
  well.
- Hangar door opens for the embarked helicopters.

## What it looks like in action

The tumblehome hull and flat-faceted deckhouse give it an almost featureless, faceted silhouette with none of the
usual mast clutter — deliberately hard to read at a distance. Missiles launch from cells spread along the deck
edge rather than a single amidships block, so a salvo appears to come from multiple points along the hull.
