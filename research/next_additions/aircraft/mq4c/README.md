# Northrop Grumman MQ-4C Triton

Role: high-altitude, long-endurance unmanned aircraft (Global Hawk derivative) flying wide-area maritime
surveillance for the carrier group — a persistent sensor layer the roster's P-8A/SH-60 sorties can't sustain
around the clock, feeding the same shared track picture as Aegis/CEC.

## Specs

- Wingspan: 39.9 m (130 ft) — [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/mq-4c-triton-bams-uas-us/)
- Length: 14.5 m (47.6 ft) — [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/mq-4c-triton-bams-uas-us/)
- Operating altitude: 50,000+ ft — [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/mq-4c-triton-bams-uas-us/)
- Endurance: 24+ hours — [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/mq-4c-triton-bams-uas-us/)
- Range: 7,400 nautical miles — [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/mq-4c-triton-bams-uas-us/)
- Powerplant: 1x Rolls-Royce F137 turbofan — [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/mq-4c-triton-bams-uas-us/)

## Visible parts / what it looks like in action

- Slender, high-aspect-ratio glider-like wing (Global Hawk family shape) on a bulbous forward fuselage housing
  the AN/ZPY-3 radar and sensor suite; a V-tail rather than a conventional cross-tail.
- No cockpit/canopy — unmanned, so the nose is a smooth radome; landing gear is a simple tricycle set, retracting
  for a very clean cruise silhouette at altitude.
- Ground operations: taxis and takes off like a slow, wide-winged aircraft — a long takeoff roll given the light
  wing loading, then a shallow climb-out toward altitude.
- In cruise it holds high and level for hours with minimal visible maneuvering — a persistent, slow-moving contact
  on a wide racetrack or grid pattern rather than a fast-moving strike silhouette.
