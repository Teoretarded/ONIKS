# Boeing P-8A Poseidon

Role: land-based, 737-derived maritime patrol and anti-submarine warfare aircraft. Extends the carrier group's
sub-hunting reach well beyond the SH-60/MH-60S helicopter screen and complements the MQ-4C Triton's wide-area
surveillance with actual weapons (torpedoes) and a large sonobuoy field.

## Specs

- Length: 39.47 m (129.5 ft) — [Boeing P-8 Poseidon](https://www.boeing.com/defense/p-8-poseidon)
- Wingspan: 37.64 m (123.6 ft) — [Boeing P-8 Poseidon](https://www.boeing.com/defense/p-8-poseidon)
- Max speed: 490 knots (~907 km/h) — [Airforce/RAAF fact sheet](https://www.airforce.gov.au/aircraft/p-8a-poseidon)
- Combat radius: 2,200+ km, with 4 hours on station — [RAAF fact sheet](https://www.airforce.gov.au/aircraft/p-8a-poseidon)
- Max takeoff weight: ~85,820 kg — [RAAF fact sheet](https://www.airforce.gov.au/aircraft/p-8a-poseidon)
- Armament: Mk 54 torpedoes, Harpoon/AGM-84, plus 129 sonobuoys internal + external — [NAVAIR](https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/P-8A-Poseidon)

## Visible parts / what it looks like in action

- Recognisably a 737 airliner airframe with military additions: an underbelly weapons bay bulge aft of the wing,
  a bomb-bay-style rotary launcher, and wing-mounted hardpoints for Harpoon/AGM-84.
- MAD-less design (no tail stinger, unlike older P-3 Orions) — a distinctive blunt tail cone is the quick
  silhouette read versus a P-3.
- In an ASW pass it opens the weapons-bay doors and ejects sonobuoys in a rapid sequential drop, each trailing a
  small parachute before splashdown — a repeating "pop-pop-pop" release pattern along the flight path.
- Torpedo drop: bay doors open, a single torpedo falls with a small drogue chute before splashdown — a distinct,
  slower release than the sonobuoy sequence.
