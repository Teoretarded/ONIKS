# Kamov Ka-52K (Katran)

Role: navalised, shipborne version of the Ka-52 Alligator attack helicopter, developed for the Mistral-class deal
and now flown from Russian amphibious/helicopter-carrying ships. Adds a rotary-wing strike option against landing
craft and coastal armour, distinct from the fixed-wing Su-34/Su-30SM2 already covering land-attack.

## Specs

- Length: 13.9 m — [Helis.com database](https://www.helis.com/database/model/Ka-52K-Katran/)
- Co-axial main rotor diameter: 14.5 m — [Helis.com database](https://www.helis.com/database/model/Ka-52K-Katran/)
- Max speed: ~290 km/h; cruise ~250 km/h — [Helis.com database](https://www.helis.com/database/model/Ka-52K-Katran/)
- Max take-off weight: ~12,200 kg — [Helis.com database](https://www.helis.com/database/model/Ka-52K-Katran/)
- Powerplant: 2x Klimov VK-2500 turboshafts, ~2,500 hp each — [Helis.com database](https://www.helis.com/database/model/Ka-52K-Katran/)
- Naval adaptations: manually-folding three-blade rotor sets (~1 minute fold/unfold) and corrosion-resistant
  marinised airframe — [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/ka-52k-katran-helicopter/)

## Visible parts / what it looks like in action

- Same twin coaxial (stacked, contra-rotating) rotor system as the land Ka-52 — no tail rotor, giving a stubby,
  symmetric silhouette very different from any single-rotor helicopter already modelled.
- Side-by-side two-seat cockpit under a heavily framed canopy, stub wings for weapon pylons and countermeasure
  dispensers, and a chin-mounted electro-optical/gun turret.
- Naval tell: shorter, manually-folded wings and rotor blades for shipboard stowage — visibly "tucked in" on deck
  versus the land Ka-52's fully spread rotor disc.
- Deck operations: folds/unfolds its blades in a slow, deliberate sequence (about a minute) before spin-up — a
  distinct pre-launch beat versus a fixed-wing catapult shot.
