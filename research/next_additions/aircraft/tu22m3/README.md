# Tupolev Tu-22M3 (Backfire)

Role: long-range, variable-sweep-wing Russian bomber — carries the heaviest anti-ship/land-attack missile loads
in the roster, at the cost of "Low" map fit (a 170x140 km coastal map is small for its operating radius).

## Specs

- Length: 42.46 m; wingspan 34.28 m spread (20° sweep) narrowing to 23.3 m swept (65° sweep); height 11.05 m — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M)
- Empty weight: 58,000 kg; max takeoff weight 126,000 kg — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M)
- Max speed: ~1,997-2,300 km/h (Mach ~1.88-2.05, sources differ); range 6,800 km — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M), [New Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M)
- Crew: 4 (pilot, co-pilot, navigator, weapon systems officer) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M)
- Two Kuznetsov NK-25 afterburning turbofans; entered service 1989 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M)

## Shape and layout

Front to back: a long, needle-fine nose radome, a stepped four-seat cockpit under a low framed canopy, a
variable-geometry wing pivoting at mid-span (glove fixed, outer panel sweeps), a semi-recessed weapons bay under
the fuselage plus underwing/centreline hardpoints for the largest missiles, two widely-spaced engines fed by
rectangular intakes on the fuselage sides, and a single large tail fin — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M).

## Moving parts

- Wings sweep from 20° (takeoff/cruise) to 65° (high-speed/low-level penetration) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-22M).
- Weapons bay doors open for internal ordnance; external pylons carry the largest missiles (e.g. Kh-22/32-class)
  semi-recessed under the fuselage.
- Multi-wheel bogie main gear (6 wheels per side in some photos) retracts into fuselage/wing-root fairings.

## What it looks like in action

The swept-wing silhouette visibly changes shape between takeoff (wings spread wide) and high-speed flight (wings
swept tight against the fuselage) — one of the few aircraft in the roster whose outline isn't fixed. Afterburner
takeoffs throw long, bright exhaust plumes from the widely-spaced engines, and it typically flies with a fighter
escort given its size and lack of self-defence armament.
