# Yasen-M class (Project 885M / Severodvinsk & Kazan)

Role: Russia's newest nuclear-powered multi-purpose (cruise-missile + attack) submarine. Lead unit *Severodvinsk*
(Project 885, 2013) and improved *Kazan* (Project 885M, 2021 on) carry the same universal 3S14 vertical cells as
the surface Kalibr/Oniks/Zircon ships — a submerged-launch complement to the roster's existing Kilo class.

## Specs

- Length: ~130 m for 885M/Yasen-M (*Kazan*) — **shorter** than the base 885's ~139.2 m (*Severodvinsk*): a
  redesigned berthing/reactor compartment shrank the hull — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasen-class_submarine)
  (fixed: previous entry had the two lengths reversed)
- Beam: ~13 m — [Missile Threat / CSIS](https://missilethreat.csis.org/russia-submarine-forces/)
- Displacement: ~13,800 tonnes submerged — [Naval News class profile](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2021/05/russias-second-yasen-m-class-submarine-kazan-commissioned/)
- Speed: ~35 knots submerged max (reported; ~28 kn on base 885) — [Naval News class profile](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2021/05/russias-second-yasen-m-class-submarine-kazan-commissioned/)
- Diving depth: ~600 m (reported) — [Naval News class profile](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2021/05/russias-second-yasen-m-class-submarine-kazan-commissioned/)
- Armament (885M/Yasen-M): 10 VLS tubes (up to 32 Kalibr/Oniks/Zircon rounds), 8 torpedo tubes — base 885 has
  8 VLS/10 torpedo tubes instead — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasen-class_submarine) (fixed: previous
  entry gave the base-885 tube counts for this Yasen-M folder)
- Commissioned hulls: *Severodvinsk* (2013), *Kazan* (2021), further boats building/trialling as of 2026 —
  [Naval News class profile](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2021/05/russias-second-yasen-m-class-submarine-kazan-commissioned/)

## Visible parts / what it looks like in action

- Single-hull teardrop form typical of modern Russian SSNs: a low, rounded sail set well forward of amidships,
  no external missile-hatch bulges visible when submerged (VLS tubes are internal, forward of the sail).
- Distinctive flattened, wide stern with X-form or cruciform control surfaces (varies by source) and a shrouded
  propulsor rather than an exposed screw, giving a smoother wake signature than the Kilo class already modelled.
- Surfaced/in-harbour views show the low sail, forward hydroplanes retracted, and a matte black hull typical of
  Russian Navy submarines — the class reads visually similar to Kilo at a distance but notably longer and with a
  flatter sail.
- No VLS launch footage of Zircon/Kalibr from a submerged Yasen-M is in the public domain; surfaced arrival and
  static-display imagery is what exists.
