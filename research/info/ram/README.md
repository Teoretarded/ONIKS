# RIM-116 RAM (Rolling Airframe Missile)

## Public specs
- Length: 9 ft 2 in / 2.79 m. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116_Rolling_Airframe_Missile)
- Diameter: 127 mm (Block 1) / 159 mm (Block 2). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116_Rolling_Airframe_Missile)
- Launch weight: ~73.5 kg (Block 1). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116_Rolling_Airframe_Missile)
- Top speed: >Mach 2. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116_Rolling_Airframe_Missile)
- Range: ~9 km (Block 1); Block 2 sources cite up to ~10 nmi. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116_Rolling_Airframe_Missile)
- Motor: single Hercules/Bermite Mk 36 solid rocket. No published burn-time figure. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116_Rolling_Airframe_Missile)
- Launcher: Mk 49 GMLS (the deck-mounted box launcher), typically loaded with 21 missiles; the whole loaded launcher weighs ~5,777 kg. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116_Rolling_Airframe_Missile)

## What a launch looks like (Mk 49 deck-mounted box launcher — NOT Mk 41 VLS)
RAM is launched from a visible above-deck box launcher (21-round drum, aimed like a turret), not from a below-deck vertical cell, so the sequence looks different from the Mk 41 rounds:
- **Launch**: no hatch to blow — the launcher is already aimed and the missile fires straight out of its open tube on the exposed launcher face. Public DVIDS/Navy stills (USS Iwo Jima 2008, 2001 launch photo — images/) show the missile leaving almost horizontally/at the launcher's elevation angle rather than dead vertical.
- **Exit speed**: very fast, short-range intercept weapon — full thrust from the instant it leaves the tube, with a compact but intense flame at the launcher face.
- **Spin/roll**: RAM's signature visual trait is that it rolls continuously in flight (hence "Rolling Airframe") — its RF seeker only measures phase interference in one plane, so constant roll lets it scan all directions. This isn't usually visible to the naked eye/camera at normal shutter speeds (it reads as a fast, spinning streak at best), but it's the reason the airframe has no fixed "up." [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116_Rolling_Airframe_Missile)
- **Pitch-over**: minimal — RAM is fired near-level at a close-in threat (anti-ship missile or aircraft), so there's no vertical-climb-then-tip-over shape like Tomahawk/SM-2; it goes almost straight to intercept course.
- **Plume**: short, bright, compact flame and light smoke puff at the launcher — much smaller-scale than a VLS launch, over in a fraction of a second on camera.
- **Disappears fast**: given the very short engagement range (~9 km) and high speed, RAM is essentially gone from frame almost immediately after launch in most public footage.

## Manoeuvrability / drag notes
No public g-load figures. RAM is built to intercept fast, maneuvering anti-ship cruise missiles at short range, implying high agility, but exact numbers aren't published. No public drag/speed-decay curve.

## Where sources disagree
- Range is given as "9 km" in some summaries and "~10 nmi" (~18.5 km) for Block 2 in others — these describe different blocks, not a single disputed figure, but they're easy to conflate.
- Diameter is sometimes quoted as a flat "127 mm" without noting the larger Block 2 (159 mm) motor.
