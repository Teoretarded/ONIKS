Diameter/length/mass match the parent AGM-84E SLAM family (Harpoon-derived airframe); SLAM-ER's main external difference is the planar, more-angular Tomahawk-style nose/seeker and clipped strake wings.

## What a launch looks like

1. **Carriage**: carried captive on a wing pylon (F/A-18C/D/E/F); wings and tail fins are folded flush against the body until release.
2. **Release**: this is a **free-fall drop**, not a rail or rocket-boosted launch — an ejector rack kicks it down and away from the wing pylon. Public sources do not give an exact drop distance/time before ignition; by comparison with the closely related Harpoon/SLAM family and typical air-launched turbojet cruise missiles, the pattern is a brief unpowered fall (on the order of 1 second, roughly 10–20 m) to clear the wing and let the airflow deploy the wings/fins before the engine lights — no public SLAM-ER-specific figure exists, note as approximate/inferred.
3. **Fin/wing deployment**: strake wings and tail fins snap open during the fall — this is the visually distinctive moment (a compact folded shape suddenly gaining a wingspan) and worth animating explicitly.
4. **Engine start**: the J402 turbojet spools up after wing deployment; unlike a rocket, there is no big ignition flash — expect a small heat-shimmer/exhaust haze and a thin, mostly transparent jet exhaust trail (turbojet, not solid rocket — no thick white smoke column).
5. **Transition to cruise**: missile noses over from the drop into level powered flight, accelerating to its ~0.7 Mach cruise speed, then flies low and level toward the target area under GPS/INS with a datalink update and terminal infrared seeker for the final run (guidance details out of scope here).
6. **Visual signature in flight**: small, fast-moving object with a faint heat-shimmer trail; not a dramatic smoke rocket — most of the visual interest is at the drop/wing-deployment/engine-light moment, not the cruise.

No Wikimedia Commons photo of an actual SLAM-ER separation/drop moment was found; only captive-carriage and static/loading photos are publicly available (see images/). Treat the drop-sequence timing above as inferred from general air-launched-cruise-missile practice, not a documented SLAM-ER figure.
