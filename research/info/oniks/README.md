# 3M55 Oniks / P-800 Yakhont (K-300P Bastion-P)

## Specs
- Length: 8.9 m (8.6 m SSM / 8.3 m ASM per some sources) — diameter 0.7 m — wingspan 1.7 m.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-800_Oniks), [Missile Threat](https://missilethreat.csis.org/missile/ss-n-26/)
- Mass: 3,000 kg launch weight (2,550 kg air-launched ASM variant). [Missile Threat]
- Two-stage: solid-fuel rocket booster for initial acceleration, then a kerosene-fuelled liquid-fuel ramjet
  ("integral rocket-ramjet" family, same lineage as BrahMos) for cruise. [Wikipedia]
- Speed: Mach 2.9 (~3,180–3,600 km/h) peak / high-altitude profile; ramjet cruise ~Mach 2.5; Missile Threat
  gives a lower ~Mach 2.2 (750 m/s) typical and Mach 2 (680 m/s) at low altitude — sources disagree on exact
  cruise Mach, but all agree it's supersonic throughout flight, unlike most subsonic cruise missiles.
- Cruise altitude: 10,000–14,000 m (both sources agree, "up to 14 km"). Terminal sea-skim: ~5–15 m (Wikipedia
  says 5 m rising to 10 m+, Missile Threat says 10–15 m) — pick ~10 m for the game, it's within both ranges.
- Bastion-P TEL (K-340P, MZKT-7930 8x8): 2 missiles/vehicle, ready to fire within 5 minutes of stopping, pair
  fired 2–5 s apart. [K-300P Bastion-P wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-300P_Bastion-P)

## What a launch looks like
1. **Container/cover**: missile rides in a sealed cylindrical transport-launch canister on the TEL bed; no
   separate nose cover callout in public sources — treat the canister front cap as blowing off/tearing at
   ignition (standard for this class, not confirmed frame-by-frame for Oniks specifically).
2. **Launch angle**: public sources call the Bastion launch "vertical" (Wikipedia's K-300P page), but photos of
   the actual TEL (see `images/`) show the canisters raised to a steep incline, not dead vertical — likely
   Wikipedia is reusing ship-VLS language loosely. Treat it as a steep incline (~70–80°) matching the reference
   photos, not a flat-vertical VLS pop-up.
3. **Ignition**: solid rocket booster ignites at/near the canister (no confirmed cold-launch gas-eject stage for
   Oniks the way S-400/Kalibr VLS do it — this is a TEL, not a silo).
4. **Boost**: booster burns rapidly, missile accelerates hard off the rail with a bright, dense white/grey solid-
   propellant plume and a loud crack — visually the closest reference is any large solid-fuel SRB launch.
5. **Booster burnout / ramjet light**: sources don't give a burn-time number. The ramjet then takes over for
   sustained supersonic cruise; expect a shorter, hotter, less smoky exhaust than the booster phase once ramjet
   thrust dominates (ramjets don't carry their own oxidizer smoke-former the way solid boosters do).
6. **Climb and level-off**: missile pitches from the launch incline over to its cruise heading and climbs to the
   10–14 km cruise band within the first tens of seconds (no public precise pitch-over timing).
7. **Cruise**: level supersonic flight at high altitude for most of the range, radar-guided.
8. **Terminal**: descends to sea-skimming altitude (~5–15 m) for the final approach at high supersonic speed
   (Mach 2+), making the last seconds of flight look flat, fast and very close to the water.

**Gaps/disagreements**: exact booster burn time, whether/how a nose or intake cover is discarded, and the
precise pitch-over timing are not documented in public sources found here — treat those as directing/animator's
choice within the bounds above rather than hard facts.

## Note
The 200–250 kg warhead and terminal guidance/seeker logic are intentionally omitted — visual reference only.
