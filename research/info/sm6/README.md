# RIM-174 SM-6 (Standard Missile 6)

## Public specs
- Length: 21.5 ft / 6.55 m (uses SM-2ER Block IV body + new seeker). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-174_Standard_ERAM)
- Diameter: 13.5 in / 34 cm (Block I/IA); Block IB widens the second stage to 21 in / 53 cm (the Mk 72 booster stays). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-174_Standard_ERAM)
- Wingspan: 42 in / 1.07 m. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-174_Standard_ERAM)
- Launch mass: ~3,300 lb / 1,500 kg. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-174_Standard_ERAM)
- Top speed: Mach 3.5 (~2,660 mph / 4,290 km/h). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-174_Standard_ERAM)
- Ceiling: >110,000 ft / 34,000 m; range ~130 nmi published (unclassified estimates run higher). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-174_Standard_ERAM)
- Two stages: Mk 72 solid-fuel booster (1st stage, shared with SM-2ER/SM-3), then a Mk 104 dual-thrust rocket motor (booster+sustainer in one case) for the 2nd stage. Mk 72 burn is commonly given as ~6 s (not officially published); no public Mk 104 figure. [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/standard-missile-6/), [designation-systems.net](https://designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-174.html)

## What a launch looks like
Fired hot (motor ignites in-cell) from Mk 41 VLS, same visual signature as SM-2 below since it shares the Mk 72 first stage:
- **Hatch**: the cell's aluminum gas-management hatch blows off explosively an instant before the missile clears the cell — visible as a small flash/kick of debris just ahead of the main plume. [Wikipedia (Mk 41 VLS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_41_vertical_launching_system)
- **Exit**: missile leaves the cell already under full booster thrust — no slow "creep" out of the tube, it's essentially instant acceleration once the hatch clears.
- **Vertical rise**: climbs straight up for roughly 2-4 seconds before any visible pitch — Navy footage (e.g. USS Savannah Oct 2023, USS John Paul Jones 2014, both in images/) shows a near-vertical column of smoke for the first several hundred feet.
- **Pitch-over**: begins a shallow lean toward the target bearing partway up the Mk 72 burn; the turn is gentle compared to Tomahawk's steep tip-over — SM-6 stays closer to vertical because it's engaging airborne, not surface, targets.
- **Plume**: thick white-grey smoke column at launch (Mk 72 booster) with a visible bright core; after Mk 72 burnout the booster case drops away and the Mk 104 stage ignites with a smaller, cleaner flame for sustained climb-out.
- **Disappears fast**: within 5-8 seconds of leaving the deck the missile is a shrinking point of light/smoke at the top of the frame in most ship-deck footage; full burnout and separation happen out of camera range on most public videos.
- **Booster drop**: the spent Mk 72 case separates and falls away; no footage in public release shows the impact, but it follows the same ballistic drop as SM-2's booster.
- **Cruise/climb profile**: steep climb-out toward altitude (>100,000 ft ceiling) rather than a cruise-missile-style level-off; SM-6 stays boost-sustain all the way to intercept, unlike ESSM/RAM's terminal maneuvering.

## Manoeuvrability / drag notes
No public g-load figures for SM-6 specifically. It inherits the SM-2ER Block IV/IVA airframe plus fins, which is described in open literature only as capable of high-g terminal maneuvers against maneuvering targets; exact numbers are not published. Speed bleeds off after Mk 104 burnout as with any unpowered/coasting missile — no public drag/deceleration curve.

## Where sources disagree
- Range: officially "130 nmi" but multiple outlets (Naval Technology, defense press) cite unofficial estimates up to 200+ nmi for extended-range engagements; treat >130 nmi as unconfirmed.
- Diameter: sources sometimes quote only 13.5 in (Block IA on the legacy Mk 72 booster) without noting the larger 21 in Block IB booster — check which variant a figure refers to.
