# AIM-120 AMRAAM (focus: AIM-120D), F/A-18E rail/ejector launch

## Public specs

| | AIM-120C-5/7 | AIM-120D |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 3.65 m (12 ft) | same, ~3.66 m |
| Diameter | 178 mm (7 in) | 178 mm (7 in) |
| Wingspan | 0.53 m (21 in) | ~0.45 m (19 in), clipped fins |
| Weight | 152 kg (335 lb) | ~162 kg (358 lb) |
| Speed | Mach 4 | Mach 4 |
| Range | ~55–75 km (30–40 nmi) | classified, est. 130–180 km (70–112 nmi) |

Sources: [Wikipedia AIM-120 AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM), [designation-systems.net](https://www.designation-systems.net/dusrm/m-120.html). AIM-120D range is not officially published; 130–180 km is the commonly cited estimate range (disagreement noted). AIM-120D has a slightly bigger motor section after the F3R upgrade (form/fit/function/refresh), but no public burn-time figure exists — treat exact burn time as unknown/classified.

Propellant: reduced-smoke HTPB (hydroxyl-terminated poly-butadiene) solid rocket motor — this is why the AMRAAM's motor plume is much fainter/thinner than older missiles like the AIM-7 Sparrow, not a thick white column.

## What a launch looks like (F/A-18E/F, LAU-127 rail or ejector rack)

1. **Release**: on wingtip/wing-station rail launches (LAU-127), the missile slides off the rail under its own rocket ignition — rail launch and motor ignition are simultaneous, no free-fall gap. On fuselage/centerline stations with an ejector rack, the missile is pneumatically/pyrotechnically kicked down and away from the aircraft first (a few feet, well clear of the airframe) before the motor lights, to avoid exhaust ingestion/airframe scorching.
2. **Ignition flash**: a short, bright, fairly clean flash/spike at the tail — reduced-smoke propellant means it's a compact flame, not a big cloud. Visible mostly in the first 0.3–0.5 s.
3. **Plume/trail**: thin, wispy, light-grey-to-white trail, noticeably less voluminous than a Sparrow/Standard missile's; distinct rocket-flame flicker only visible in the first second, then a fading smoke thread that drifts and disperses quickly.
4. **Acceleration**: extremely fast — it pulls ahead of the launch aircraft within roughly a second or two, visibly outrunning the jet almost immediately (this is the signature "AMRAAM snap" seen in release footage, e.g. the F/A-18F Point Mugu test clip).
5. **Burn/burnout**: rocket motor is a boost-sustain design; burns for several seconds then goes dark/unpowered, missile continues on momentum with no visible plume, so most of the missile's flight (especially at range) is smoke-trail-free and hard to see.
6. **Flight shape**: after separation it self-rights and flies essentially straight/level off the rail (no big loft/climb needed for typical intercepts — this is an air-to-air missile, not a lofted SAM), correcting toward its guidance solution within the first couple of seconds.

No public numeric figures exist for rail-clearance distance, exact ignition delay, or motor burn duration — these are not published; the above is drawn from consistent visual description across release/test footage.

## Manoeuvrability

No public G-load or turn-rate figures for the AIM-120 are published by the manufacturer; only qualitative claims ("high-G intercepts," "expanded no-escape envelope" for -D) appear in open sources.
