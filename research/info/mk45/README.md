# 5"/62 Mk 45 gun (naval gun, not a missile)

## Public specs
- Note: the 62-caliber Mod 4 is the modern variant; most public spec pages primarily document the older 54-caliber Mod 1/2, which shares the same look/behavior when firing. Figures below are Mod 2 (54-cal) unless marked otherwise.
- Barrel length (54-cal, Mod 2): 22 ft 6 in / 6.858 m; Mod 4 (62-cal) extends to ~7.87 m. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5%22/54_caliber_Mark_45_gun)
- Overall mount length: ~29 ft 6 in / 8.99 m. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5%22/54_caliber_Mark_45_gun)
- Mount weight (Mod 2): ~21,691 kg / 47,820 lb. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5%22/54_caliber_Mark_45_gun)
- Standard projectile: 31.75 kg / 70 lb (127 x 835 mm shell); sample HE-PD round ~30.7 kg / 67.6 lb. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5%22/54_caliber_Mark_45_gun)
- Muzzle velocity: ~2,500 ft/s / 760 m/s standard charge (reduced-charge option ~1,500 ft/s / 457 m/s). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5%22/54_caliber_Mark_45_gun)
- Rate of fire: 16-20 rounds/minute automatic. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5%22/54_caliber_Mark_45_gun)
- Range: ~13 nmi / 24 km (Mod 2, 54-cal); ~20 nmi / 37 km (Mod 4, 62-cal, with extended-range guided munitions). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5%22/54_caliber_Mark_45_gun)
- Magazine: automatic loader holds 20 ready rounds; can fire the full magazine in a little over a minute at max rate.

## What firing looks like
- **Muzzle flash**: a single sharp, bright white-orange flash at the muzzle each shot, visible day or night; night footage (search links.md) shows it as a brief, intense flare that lights up the surrounding deck/turret.
- **Smoke**: a compact puff of white-grey smoke follows each shot and disperses quickly in the wind over the bow; sustained rapid fire builds a low haze around the mount rather than one big cloud.
- **Recoil / mount motion**: the barrel visibly recoils (short, fast jolt back into the mount) after each round; the whole turret can be seen to shudder slightly under rapid fire, and the barrel returns to firing position almost immediately given the 16-20 rpm rate.
- **Rate/rhythm on camera**: at max rate (16-20 rpm) shots are roughly 3-4 seconds apart — footage shows a steady, mechanical firing rhythm rather than a rapid stutter (compare to a CIWS/Phalanx's buzz-saw rate, which is completely different).
- **Ejected casings/gas**: modern rounds are mostly casings-consumed/combustible-case or metal-cased depending on round type; public footage doesn't clearly show casing ejection, the visible action is the muzzle flash/smoke and barrel recoil cycle.
- **Daylight vs low light**: in bright daylight the flash reads mostly as smoke + a brief white flare; at dusk/night the flash dominates the frame and is the primary visual cue of each shot (see USS Bainbridge images in images/, one daylight practice-round shot and one different-lighting firing shot).

## Manoeuvrability / drag notes
Not applicable in the missile sense — this is a naval gun. No further "g-load"/drag data applies; muzzle velocity above is the relevant "top speed" figure, and the projectile sheds velocity ballistically after leaving the barrel (standard artillery drag, no public deceleration curve specific to this round).

## Where sources disagree
- Most easily-findable specs (including this Wikipedia page) are for the 5"/54 Mod 1/2, not the 5"/62 Mod 4 that's more common on current Arleigh Burke Flight IIA+ ships; where a figure differs between calibers it's noted above, but a dedicated 62-cal spec sheet would be more precise for exact barrel length/range.
