# 9M133 Kornet

## Specs
- Length: 1.2 m (missile only; 9M134 Bulat sub-variant ~0.83 m). Diameter: 152 mm warhead/body calibre, 160 mm
  launch tube. Mass: 27 kg missile / 29 kg with launch tube; full tripod system 63.7 kg.
  [9M133 Kornet — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9M133_Kornet)
- Speed: >250 m/s baseline, later variants 300–320 m/s.
- Range: 100–5,500 m standard (Kornet-EM extends to 8,000–10,000 m).
- Launch method: **soft launch** — the missile is ejected from the tube and the rocket motor only ignites once
  it's clear, at a safe distance from the launcher/operator (standard for shoulder/tripod-fired ATGMs, keeps
  backblast and heat away from the crew). [Soft launch (missile) — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_launch_(missile))
- Guidance: SACLOS laser beam-riding — the operator keeps the sight on the target throughout flight; two
  missiles can be guided at once on separate laser channels from vehicle-mounted launchers.

## What a launch looks like
1. **Tube**: missile sits sealed in its disposable launch tube, mounted on the tripod (infantry) or vehicle
   launcher rail.
2. **Soft eject**: a small ejection charge pushes the missile out of the tube with little flash/smoke at the
   operator's position — this is the "soft launch" step, deliberately quiet/clean compared to a rocket that
   ignites inside the tube.
3. **Motor ignition in the air**: once a short safe distance clear of the tube, the main solid rocket motor
   ignites — this is where the visible flash, smoke plume and roar actually start, some distance downrange of
   the operator/vehicle.
4. **Flight**: fin-stabilised flight along the laser beam toward the target; wingspan 460 mm control surfaces
   deploy after launch. Flight is fast enough that engagement-to-impact is only a few seconds at typical combat
   ranges (100 m/s+ over ranges up to a few km means single-digit seconds for most engagements).
5. **Impact**: tandem-charge detonation on impact (visual only — no internals needed here).
6. **Operator view**: because it's SACLOS, the operator/sight stays pointed at the target the whole flight — no
   separate "fire and forget" break in attention; camera/sight shake or tracking motion is part of the shot.

**Gaps**: no sourced numbers for eject-to-ignition distance/time, or exhaust plume colour/length specifically
for Kornet (soft-launch ATGMs in general keep this short — on the order of a few metres/tenths of a second —
but that figure isn't confirmed Kornet-specific here).
