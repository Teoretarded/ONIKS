# CVN-68 Nimitz-class aircraft carrier (nuclear-powered)

## Dimensions and mass
- Length overall: 1,092 ft (332.8 m); waterline length ~1,040 ft (317 m).
- Beam (flight deck, max): 252 ft (76.8 m); waterline beam ~134 ft (40.8 m).
- Height: keel to top of mast ~244 ft (74 m); flight deck sits ~20 m (~65 ft) above the waterline.
- Draft: ~37 ft (11.3 m).
- Displacement: ~100,000–104,000 long tons full load (class grows hull to hull; Nimitz herself ~100,020 LT).
- Flight deck area: ~4.5 acres (18,200 m²).
- Crew: ~3,200 ship's company + ~2,480 air wing when embarked (~5,000–5,680 total).
Sources: [Wikipedia, Nimitz-class aircraft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimitz-class_aircraft_carrier), [CNRNW Nimitz fact sheet PDF](https://cnrnw.cnic.navy.mil/Portals/82/CNRNW/Documents/NIMITZ%20Fact%20sheet.pdf), [NVR ship detail CVN 68](https://www.nvr.navy.mil/nvr/getHull.htm?shipId=5151).

## Speed / propulsion
- 2x A4W pressurized-water nuclear reactors, 4 shafts, ~260,000 shp.
- Max speed: 30+ kt (officially "30+"; commonly cited 31.5 kt).
- Reactor core life: ~20–25 years (one mid-life refueling, RCOH).
Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimitz-class_aircraft_carrier), [GlobalSecurity CVN-68](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/ship/cvn-68.htm).

## Shape and layout (bow to stern)
- **Bow**: flush flight deck overhang, anchor hawseholes low on both sides, bow sonar dome not fitted (surface combatant only — no bow sonar on a carrier).
- **Bow catapults**: two steam catapults (Cat 1 & 2) side by side on the bow, angled slightly outboard; jet blast deflectors (JBDs) flush-mounted behind each.
- **Forward flight-deck edge**: forward elevator (deck-edge, starboard, forward of the island) and a second forward elevator port side, forward of the bow catapults' run.
- **Angled deck**: ~9° offset landing area on the port-aft side with two waist catapults (Cat 3 & 4) and 3–4 arresting wires; net/barricade stanchions stowed at the ramp.
- **Island**: starboard side, roughly amidships-aft; stacked bridge (navigation bridge below, primary flight control "Pri-Fly" above with tall windows), SPS-48/SPS-49 air-search radars and later AN/SPQ-9B, electronic warfare antennas, and the ship's two funnels/stacks integrated into the island structure (Nimitz-class exhausts through the island, unlike oil-fired carriers' separate stacks).
- **Elevators**: 4 deck-edge elevators total — 2 forward (port and starboard, ahead of the island) and 2 aft (starboard, both aft of the island) — each lowers to the hangar deck, ~1 aircraft at a time (~26 x 16 m platform).
- **Hangar deck**: one large open bay below the flight deck, subdivided by fire curtains, running most of the ship's length.
- **CIWS/self-defense**: 3x Phalanx 20mm CIWS mounts (typically 2 forward corners, 1 aft) plus RIM-116 RAM launchers replacing some Sea Sparrow NATO launchers on refit ships — positions vary by hull and refit.
- **Aft flight deck / fantail**: round-down ramp, tie-down deck for parked aircraft, stern lacks a well deck (not an amphib).

## Moving parts
- 4x steam catapults, ~2–3 second stroke, launch interval as low as 20 sec/cat under surge ops.
- 4x arresting wires (Mk 7 gear), aircraft caught within ~2–3 seconds.
- 4x deck-edge elevators, ~sec-scale traverse, several seconds to cycle hangar-to-flight-deck.
- SPS-48E 3D air search radar rotates ~ a few rpm (mechanically scanned, typically 6 rpm-class); SPS-49 2D radar ~6 rpm; both atop the island mast.
No public precise RPM figures for shipboard air-search radars are published by the Navy; the 6 rpm figure is the commonly cited SPS-48/49 rotation rate from open literature — treat as approximate.

## Markings / colours
- Haze grey hull and superstructure (Navy standard 5-H grey types), flight deck non-skid dark grey-brown/black-red with yellow/white catapult and foul-line markings, elevator numbers painted large on the deck-edge lifts, hull number (e.g. "68") painted in white/black on the bow and island.

Note: displacement, exact elevator dimensions, and radar rotation rates vary slightly across published sources (Navy fact sheets vs. Jane's vs. manufacturer data); figures above are the commonly-cited consensus values with disagreement flagged.
