# Project 636.3 (Improved Kilo class, NATO "Kilo"), diesel-electric attack submarine

> **Correction (checked against the photos, 2026-09-25):** Kilo diving planes are on the bow casing, not on the sail; the sail-plane text below is wrong.

Single-hull diesel-electric submarine, export/Russian-Navy-improved version of Project 877. Russian Black Sea
Fleet operates 6 (Novorossiysk-class boats, incl. "Rostov-on-Don"); export customers include Algeria, Vietnam,
China, India, Iran, Myanmar.

Speed and range figures vary noticeably between sources (submerged speed cited anywhere from 17 to 25 kn) — treat as approximate; exact figures are not publicly confirmed by the Russian Navy.

## Shape and layout

Classic teardrop single-hull submarine form: rounded bow with bow-plane fairings (retractable diving planes,
low on the hull near the bow), long parallel-sided mid-body, tapering stern with a cruciform tail (rudder +
stern planes) around a single 6-blade propeller. Prominent tall sail (fin) roughly amidships-forward, with
fairwater/sail-mounted diving planes partway up the sail on Project 636.3 boats; sail houses periscopes,
snorkel mast, radio/ESM masts (all retractable, visible as raised masts only when surfaced/snorkelling).
Hull is featureless black/dark-grey steel with no external ordnance visible (torpedo tubes are internal, bow-
mounted, not visible externally). Deck line runs flat and low along the top of the pressure hull from bow to
sail; limber holes (drainage slots) visible along the casing in close-up photos.

## Moving parts

- **Sail-mounted and bow diving planes**: rotate to control pitch/depth.
- **Stern cruciform (rudder + planes)**: control yaw and depth aft.
- **Periscope/snorkel/mast cluster** in the sail: raise and lower (retractable), visible extended only when
  near the surface.
- **Single propeller**: 6/7-blade skewed screw for low cavitation noise (exact blade count varies by source).

## Colours / camouflage

Overall matte black or very dark grey anechoic-tile-covered hull (standard for all Kilo/Improved Kilo boats);
red/orange boot-topping stripe at the waterline visible when surfaced or in dry dock.

## Disagreements / gaps

Submerged speed and displacement figures vary between Russian, Western intelligence, and manufacturer
(Admiralty Shipyards / Rubin design bureau) sources; no fully authoritative public figure exists. Exact test
depth is classified; the ~300-350 m figures are commonly cited estimates, not official.
