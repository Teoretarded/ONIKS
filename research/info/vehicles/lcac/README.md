# LCAC Landing Craft, Air Cushion

## Dimensions and mass
- Length: 87.9 ft (26.8 m); LCAC 100-class (SSC replacement) is similar size.
- Beam/width: 47 ft (14.3 m).
- Height (skirt inflated, on cushion): ~20 ft (6.1 m) with skirt up; craft "sits" much lower with skirt deflated.
- Weight (empty): ~87,000 lb (~39.5 t); max gross weight ~170,000–200,000 lb (77–91 t) fully loaded, payload ~60–75 short tons depending on variant.
- Draft: essentially zero on cushion (a few inches); this is the entire point of an air-cushion craft — it rides above the water/beach on a bubble of air, not displacement.
Sources: [Wikipedia, LCAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Craft_Air_Cushion), [Navy fact file, LCAC](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2169587/landing-craft-air-cushion-lcac/).

## Speed / propulsion
- 4x marine gas turbines (Avco Lycoming TF40B on original LCAC) driving 2x reversible-pitch air propellers (aft-mounted, large ducted fans) for propulsion, plus separate lift fans that pressurize the rubber skirt/cushion.
- Max speed: 40+ kt over water (officially "40+ knots"; can also transit over land/marsh/ice on the cushion at reduced speed).
- Range: ~200 nmi at full load and speed.
Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Craft_Air_Cushion).

## Shape and layout (bow to stern)
- **Bow**: wide, blunt, boxy ramp — a full-width bow ramp lowers for vehicle loading/unloading, matching the shape of a well-deck stern gate.
- **Cargo deck**: flat, open, nearly the full length and width of the craft, cargo tie-down points across the deck for vehicles (can carry an M1 Abrams tank, LAVs, trucks, or palletized cargo/troops).
- **Skirt**: a deep, flexible rubber/fabric skirt runs around the entire perimeter of the hull, inflated by the lift-fan air to contain the air cushion — this skirt is the single most visually distinctive feature, giving the craft a much taller "walled" silhouette when cushion-borne than its hull alone.
- **Superstructure/control station**: offset to one side (typically port), a small enclosed control cab elevated above the cargo deck for the crew (2 pilots + 2 crew typical) to see over cargo.
- **Engine/fan pods**: mounted in raised pods/sponsons on both sides toward the stern, each housing a gas turbine, a lift fan, and driving one of the two big propulsion propellers at the very stern.
- **Propulsors**: 2x large, shrouded, reversible-pitch air propellers at the stern (not underwater propellers — this is an air-cushion vehicle, propulsion is entirely aerial/fan-driven), can pivot for steering.
- **Stern ramp**: matching ramp at the stern for drive-through loading (bow-in, stern-out or vice versa depending on the operation).

## Moving parts
- Lift fans continuously pressurize the skirt to maintain the air cushion while operating — a constant low hum/roar, not intermittent.
- 2x stern propulsors are large, visibly spinning ducted props, pivot to vector thrust for steering (no rudder in the traditional sense).
- Bow and stern ramps raise/lower hydraulically for vehicle loading.
- Skirt itself flexes/deforms continuously riding over wave and beach terrain — a visibly "bouncing/rippling" fabric edge distinct from a rigid hull.

## Markings / colours
- Haze grey topsides, black/dark grey rubber skirt, hull number stencilled on the bow ramp or side pod in white, small unit markings (Assault Craft Unit "ACU" designations) near the control cab.

Note: exact payload and gross-weight figures vary between the LCAC and newer SSC (Ship-to-Shore Connector, "LCAC 100-class") variants — the SSC has more powerful engines and different max load; figures above are representative for the classic LCAC in Navy service. Skirt height and exact "on cushion" ride height are not tightly published — treat the ~20 ft skirt-up figure as approximate.
