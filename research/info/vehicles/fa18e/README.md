# F/A-18E Super Hornet (single-seat)

## Dimensions and mass
- Length: 60.3 ft (18.31 m).
- Wingspan: 44.9 ft (13.68 m); folded ~30.6 ft (9.32 m) for shipboard stowage.
- Height: 16.0 ft (4.88 m).
- Empty weight: ~32,081 lb (14,552 kg); max takeoff weight: ~66,000 lb (29,937 kg).
- Speed: Mach 1.6+ (~1,190 mph / 1,915 km/h) at altitude; carrier approach speed ~130–140 kt.
- Combat radius: ~390–450 nmi depending on load/mission.
- Crew: 1 (Echo/single-seat); the two-seat variant is the F/A-18F.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Boeing F/A-18E/F Super Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_F/A-18E/F_Super_Hornet), [Navy fact file, F/A-18 Hornet/Super Hornet](https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2169795/fa-18-hornet-and-super-hornet-strike-fighter/), [Boeing F/A-18 product page](https://www.boeing.com/defense/f-a-18-super-hornet).

## Speed / propulsion
- 2x General Electric F414-GE-400 afterburning turbofans, ~22,000 lbf (98 kN) thrust each with afterburner.
- Max speed: Mach 1.8 class (official figure commonly listed as "Mach 1.6+"; exact top speed classified/varies by source).
Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_F/A-18E/F_Super_Hornet).

## Shape and layout (nose to tail)
- **Nose**: pointed radome housing the AN/APG-79 AESA radar (on most Block II+ aircraft), pitot boom often absent/small compared to legacy Hornet.
- **Cockpit**: single bubble canopy, HUD, glass cockpit with large multi-function displays.
- **Leading-edge extensions (LEX)**: large, distinctive strakes running from the cockpit sides forward to the wing root — the Hornet family's signature vortex-generating shape, larger/reshaped on the Super Hornet versus the legacy Hornet.
- **Engines/intakes**: rectangular, slightly caret-shaped intakes on each side of the fuselage below the LEX, feeding the two F414 engines buried in the aft fuselage; twin exhaust nozzles at the tail, close together.
- **Wings**: mid-mounted, swept, with folding outer wing panels (fold point roughly mid-span) for shipboard stowage; wingtip missile rail (typically AIM-9 Sidewinder) plus underwing pylons (typically 5 wet stations per wing area combined with fuselage stations).
- **Twin tails**: canted outward (not vertical like legacy Hornet's near-vertical fins — Super Hornet tails are visibly more raked/canted), each with a rudder; ventral strakes below the tail root.
- **Fuselage stations**: centerline and fuselage pylons for fuel tanks/weapons, in addition to wing stations.
- **Arresting hook**: retractable tailhook under the tail for carrier landings.
- **Undercarriage**: nose gear with catapult launch bar (connects to the catapult shuttle), twin main gear retracting into the fuselage/wing root.

## Moving parts
- Wings fold hydraulically at the mid-span hinge for deck stowage (a several-second, not instant, hydraulic fold/unfold).
- Tailhook drops/retracts for carrier recovery.
- Catapult launch bar on the nose gear engages the catapult shuttle before launch; nose gear "kneels"/extends during the catapult stroke (the strut stretches as the catapult fires).
- Leading/trailing-edge flaps, ailerons, and twin canted rudders move for flight control; no rotating radar dish (fixed AESA panel behind the radome).

## Markings / colours
- Standard low-visibility grey (two-tone light/dark grey) US Navy tactical paint scheme, small unit/squadron markings, "NAVY" stencil small on the fuselage, side number on the nose and tail, national insignia low-visibility grey-on-grey.

Note: exact top speed, combat radius, and thrust figures vary between official Navy/Boeing figures and secondary sources depending on load/config; figures above are the commonly cited ranges. Radar fit (APG-73 mechanical vs. APG-79 AESA) differs by production block/upgrade status.
