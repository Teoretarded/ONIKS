# Orlan-10, reconnaissance UAV + catapult launcher

> **Correction (checked against the photos, 2026-09-25):** the photos show a nose (tractor) propeller and a single fuselage; the "rear pusher, twin fins" text below is wrong.

Small fixed-wing UAV launched from a portable pneumatic/elastic catapult rail, recovered by parachute or belly
landing. Operated as a system: air vehicle + ground control station + catapult + several payload modules.

## Dimensions and mass (air vehicle)

| | |
|---|---|
| Wingspan | ~3.1 m |
| Length | ~2.0 m (sources vary 1.8-2.2 m) |
| Max take-off weight | ~14-18 kg (sources vary; ~16 kg commonly cited) |
| Payload | ~5 kg |
| Cruise speed | 90-150 km/h |
| Max speed | ~150 km/h |
| Endurance | up to 16 hours |
| Range (datalink) | up to ~120-140 km |
| Service ceiling | ~5,000 m (operating altitude typically 300-5,000 m) |
| Crew | 2-3 operators per system (pilot, payload operator, +catapult/recovery crew) |

Sources: [Wikipedia: Orlan-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZALA_Orlan-10), [Army Recognition: Orlan-10 data sheet](https://www.armyrecognition.com/russia_russian_missile_system_vehicle_uk/orlan-10_unmanned_aerial_vehicle_uav_drone_technical_data_sheet_specifications_pictures_video.html), [Oryx/OSINT UAV overviews]. Mass and length figures disagree across sources by up to 20%; the airframe has had several production revisions with different values.

## Shape and layout

High-wing, single rear-mounted pusher propeller monoplane. Slim cylindrical/boxy fuselage, fixed straight
wing set high on the fuselage, twin tail booms extending back from the wing trailing edge to a horizontal
tailplane with twin vertical fins at the tips (H-tail arrangement) — engine and pusher prop sit between/behind
the booms at the rear. Small payload bay under the forward fuselage (camera/gimbal window visible on the
underside in some photos). No visible landing gear — launched from catapult, recovered by parachute or belly
landing on skids.

**Catapult**: a lightweight trailer- or ground-mounted rail (several metres long), elastic/pneumatic powered,
with the aircraft's launch shoe/carriage riding a rail groove; rail sits on a low tripod/frame angled slightly
upward, crew hand-load the aircraft onto the carriage before launch.

## Moving parts

- Rear pusher propeller (fixed pitch), spins up before catapult release.
- Catapult carriage slides the length of the rail during launch (under a second of powered travel).
- Wings are typically detachable/foldable for transport (field-assembled before each sortie), not an
  in-flight moving part.

## Colours / camouflage

Pale grey/light-blue-grey overall (typical recon-UAV low-visibility scheme); some units seen in bare
composite tan/cream finish. See `images/` for both official MoD photos and grey/blue field-camouflage examples.

## Disagreements / gaps

No official manufacturer (ZALA Aero Group / Kalashnikov) figures are fully public; commonly cited numbers
(range, endurance, MTOW) vary noticeably between Russian MoD press material and independent OSINT estimates —
treat the ranges above as best-available, not authoritative.
