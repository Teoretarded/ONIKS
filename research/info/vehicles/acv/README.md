# ACV-1.1 Amphibious Combat Vehicle (BAE Systems/Iveco)

## Dimensions and mass
- Length: ~9.0 m (29.5 ft).
- Width: ~3.1 m (10.2 ft).
- Height: ~2.7–3.0 m (8.9–9.8 ft) depending on turret/RWS fit.
- Weight (combat loaded): ~54,000–60,000 lb (~24.5–27 metric tons, varies by source/variant).
- Crew + troops: 3 crew (commander, driver, gunner) + up to 13 embarked Marines (personnel variant, ACV-P).
Sources: [Wikipedia, BAE Systems/Iveco Amphibious Combat Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_Combat_Vehicle), [Marine Corps ACV program page](https://www.candp.marines.mil/), [BAE Systems ACV product page](https://www.baesystems.com/en-us/product/amphibious-combat-vehicle-acv).

## Speed / propulsion
- Wheeled 8x8 configuration, diesel engine, waterjet propulsion for swim (in addition to wheel drive on land).
- Land speed: ~65 mph (105 km/h) on road.
- Water speed: ~6 kt (open-ocean swim capability, ship-to-shore, unlike the tracked AAV7 it replaces which was slower in the water).
Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_Combat_Vehicle).

## Shape and layout (front to rear)
- **Hull/bow**: boat-shaped, V-hull front for mine/IED blast deflection and for good water entry/wave-splitting when swimming; a trim vane can be raised at the bow for swim stability (deploys before water entry, folds down for road travel).
- **Driver's station**: front, slightly left or center depending on variant, with day/night vision blocks.
- **Weapons station**: turret or remote weapon station (RWS) on the roof — the base personnel variant (ACV-P) typically mounts an RWS (e.g., M2 .50-cal or Mk19 grenade launcher); a separate ACV-30 variant carries a manned turret with a 30mm cannon.
- **Troop compartment**: rear two-thirds of the hull, bench seating along the sides for embarked Marines, rear-opening powered ramp (not a side door) for egress — a major change from the AAV7's ramp-and-door arrangement, sized for faster mass egress.
- **8 wheels**: large run-flat tires, all independently sprung, central tire inflation system (CTIS) lets the crew adjust tire pressure for terrain (soft sand vs. road) from inside.
- **Waterjets**: 2x waterjet units at the rear/underside of the hull for swim propulsion, distinct from a tracked AAV7's water-jet/track-thrust arrangement.
- **Variants**: ACV-P (personnel carrier, baseline), ACV-C (command and control, extra antennas/workstations, fewer troop seats), ACV-R (recovery, winch/crane fit for vehicle recovery), ACV-30 (up-gunned 30mm turret variant).

## Moving parts
- Bow trim vane raises/lowers for swim vs. road configuration.
- Rear ramp powers open/closed (hydraulic), much faster than manual AAV7-style doors.
- CTIS lets tire pressure change while underway/stationary without stopping to manually adjust.
- Waterjets engage for swim propulsion, wheel drive disengages/idles in water.
- RWS or manned turret trains/elevates; no rotating radar (a land/amphibious combat vehicle, not equipped with air-search radar).

## Markings / colours
- Marine Corps tactical green/tan/brown or solid green depending on unit paint scheme, small bumper numbers/unit markings, USMC star-and-bar low-visibility insignia.

Note: exact weight and height figures vary by publicly cited source and by variant (ACV-P vs ACV-C vs ACV-R vs ACV-30 differ in topside fit and weight); treat the figures above as representative baseline (ACV-P) values, not variant-specific.
