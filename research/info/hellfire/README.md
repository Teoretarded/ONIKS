|---|---|---|

No public burn-time figure for the TX-657 boost-sustain motor was found; open sources only describe it as boost-sustain (fast initial acceleration, then a lower-thrust sustain phase to Mach 1.3). Treat exact burn time as unpublished.

## What a launch looks like

1. **Rail**: fired from an M299/M272-type launcher rail on the MH-60R's stub wings (up to 8 missiles, 4 per side on two quad launchers).
2. **Ignition**: motor lights on the rail — no free-fall/drop phase (this is a rocket-boosted rail launch, unlike the SLAM-ER's ejector drop).
3. **Plume/smoke**: propellant is a *minimum-smoke* formulation — noticeably cleaner and less voluminous than older bigger rockets. Commons/press description calls it a relatively "clear" or thin exhaust compared to missiles with billowing white-cloud trails (this is a known, commented-on visual trait — see Quora sourcing below, treat as informal but consistent with the propellant type).
4. **Boost flash**: short, bright ignition flash at the rail, then a fast pull-away from the helicopter — the missile visibly outruns the aircraft within a fraction of a second given Mach 1.3 terminal speed from a hover/low-speed helicopter.
5. **Smoke trail**: thin, wispy trail that's much less prominent than a classic rocket-artillery/CIWS-style trail; in daylight footage it reads as a faint straight grey-white thread; the flame itself is brief.
6. **Flight shape**: after boost the missile transitions to sustain thrust and flies a largely straight line to target (semi-active laser or millimeter-wave guidance keeps it on a direct terminal path — guidance logic out of scope here); no big loft arc like a SAM.
7. **Helicopter platform effect**: because the MH-60R is often slow or hovering, the plume/rail-smoke and rotor-wash interaction is visible in most real footage — expect the exhaust smoke to be caught and dispersed by rotor downwash rather than trailing cleanly behind a fast jet.

## Manoeuvrability

No public G-load/turn-rate figures are published; only CEP (accuracy) and top speed are documented.
