# 48N6E3 (S-400 Triumf, cold vertical launch)

## Specs
- Diameter: 0.515 m (51.5 cm). [S-400 missile system — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-400_missile_system)
- Mass: 1,835 kg launch weight (180 kg warhead). [Wikipedia]
- Max missile speed: 2,000 m/s (Mach 5.9). Range 240 km, max altitude 27 km. [Wikipedia]
- Solid-fuel motor; semi-active radar homing (guidance omitted here, visual only).
  [48N6E — Astronautix](http://www.astronautix.com/4/48n6e.html)
- Length not confirmed for 48N6E3 specifically in sources checked here (earlier 48N6/48N6E family is commonly
  cited around 7.5 m — treat as approximate, not sourced from the pages fetched for this doc).

## What a launch looks like
1. **Cold launch**: a gas system (compressed gas / gas-generator, not the rocket motor) ejects the missile
   straight up out of its launch tube — no flame or exhaust visible yet. [Wikipedia: "a gas system launches
   missiles from launch tubes"]
2. **Ejection**: missile rises on gas pressure alone; general cold-launch literature puts ejection speed in the
   ~100–150 m/s range for this class of system (not 48N6E3-specific, but the standard figure for Russian cold-
   launch VLS/TELs). [Sensitivity Analysis and Flight Tests Results for a Vertical Cold Launch Missile System — MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/7/12/168)
3. **Ignition point**: main rocket motor ignites once the missile is "several meters" above the launcher and in
   the correct attitude — for the 48N6E family specifically, one source gives ignition at 30 m downrange/altitude.
   [Astronautix 48N6E]
4. **Gas-jet vector**: before/at ignition the missile uses gas-dynamic control (jet vanes/gas rudders) to tip
   itself over toward the target bearing — this is what gives cold-launch SAMs their distinctive "pop up, hang,
   then snap over and streak off at an angle" look, unlike a rocket that thrusts off the rail already aimed.
5. **Boost and climb**: once lit, the solid motor burns hard and the missile accelerates rapidly toward its
   target track, climbing to intercept altitude (up to 27 km).
6. **Visual result**: silent vertical pop-up (no flame) for roughly a second, then ignition, a sharp tip-over,
   and a fast climb-out — very different from a rail/TEL launch where the flame and motion start together from
   ground level.
