# RIM-162 ESSM (Evolved Sea Sparrow Missile)

## Public specs
- Length: 12 ft / 3.66 m. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-162_ESSM)
- Diameter: 10 in / 254 mm. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-162_ESSM)
- Weight: 620 lb / 280 kg. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-162_ESSM)
- Top speed: Mach 4+. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-162_ESSM)
- Range: 27 nmi+ / 50 km+ (Block 2 goes further; exact figure not public). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-162_ESSM)
- Motor: single Mk 134 Mod 0 solid rocket (Nammo Raufoss/Orbital ATK), larger-diameter than the original Sea Sparrow's Mk 58 motor for more range and speed. No published burn-time figure. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-162_ESSM)
- Quad-packed: four ESSM canisters fit in a single Mk 41 VLS cell (vs. one Standard Missile per cell), so a launch typically shows one round leaving from a cell also holding three others. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-162_ESSM)

## What a launch looks like (Mk 41 VLS, hot launch)
- **Hatch**: same hot-launch hatch-blow sequence as the Standard missiles — the quad-pack canister's own hatch opens explosively just ahead of missile exit. [Wikipedia (Mk 41 VLS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_41_vertical_launching_system)
- **Exit**: full thrust immediately on clearing the canister, same instant-acceleration look as SM-2/SM-6, but from a visibly smaller/thinner airframe and canister than a full-size Standard Missile round.
- **Vertical rise**: shorter and less prolonged vertical run than SM-2/SM-6 in most footage (e.g. RIM-162 launched from USS Carl Vinson, 2010 — image in images/) — ESSM's thrust-vectoring lets it start turning hard much sooner.
- **Pitch-over / turn**: distinctive — ESSM uses tail-fin thrust-vector control for a very sharp, fast turn onto an intercept course almost immediately off the rail (visibly different from the slower Standard Missile lean-over), since it's designed for short-range, high-angle engagements against close/maneuvering threats.
- **Plume**: brighter, tighter flame and less voluminous smoke column than SM-2/SM-6, consistent with the smaller Mk 134 motor; still a clear white-grey trail against open sky in daylight shots.
- **Disappears fast**: given the shorter engagement ranges ESSM is built for, it crosses out of typical ship-camera frame within a couple of seconds of the sharp turn.
- **Cruise/climb profile**: not a cruise weapon — flies a short, high-g intercept profile rather than any climb-and-level pattern; whole engagement (launch to impact) is seconds, not the tens of seconds a Standard Missile intercept takes.

## Manoeuvrability / drag notes
ESSM's headline feature versus legacy Sea Sparrow is much higher end-game maneuverability from combined aerodynamic + thrust-vector control, letting it handle steep, high-speed crossing targets; no public g-load number is published. No public drag/speed-decay curve.

## Where sources disagree
- Public range figures vary from "27 nmi+" (conservative, older Block 1 sourcing) to unofficial estimates of 30-50 km depending on Block 1 vs Block 2; Missile Threat/CSIS and Wikipedia give slightly different numbers — treat any exact range as approximate.
