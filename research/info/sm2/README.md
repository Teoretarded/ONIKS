# RIM-66 SM-2 (Standard Missile 2, medium range)

## Public specs
- Length: 15 ft 6 in / 4.72 m. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-66_Standard)
- Diameter: 13.5 in / 34.3 cm; wingspan 3 ft 6 in / 1.07 m. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-66_Standard)
- Mass: 1,558 lb / 707 kg. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-66_Standard)
- Top speed: Mach 3.5 (~2,660 mph / 4,290 km/h). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-66_Standard)
- Ceiling: >82,000 ft / 25,000 m; range 40-92 nmi depending on block. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-66_Standard)
- Propulsion: dual-thrust solid rocket motor; Block II introduced the Thiokol Mk 104 motor, nearly doubling range over earlier blocks. No published exact burn-time figures for the booster phase. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-66_Standard)
- The separate-booster VLS/rail versions used the Mk 72 first-stage booster (same booster shared with SM-6 and SM-3) to extend range beyond the self-boosting MR round; burn time not publicly listed.

## What a launch looks like (Mk 41 VLS, hot launch)
- **Hatch**: cell hatch blows explosively a beat before the missile clears the tube — a quick flash/debris kick precedes the main plume. [Wikipedia (Mk 41 VLS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_41_vertical_launching_system)
- **Exit speed**: missile is already at full booster thrust the instant it clears the hatch — no slow creep, straight to a fast vertical shot.
- **Vertical rise**: DVIDS/Navy footage (USS Higgins, USS Shoup, USS Monterey — see links.md) shows the missile holding a near-vertical line for roughly the first 2-3 seconds and several hundred feet before any visible lean.
- **Pitch-over**: gentle turn toward the target bearing begins as the booster burns down; SM-2 (like SM-6) stays closer to vertical for longer than a cruise missile since it's chasing an airborne target, not skimming to a surface course.
- **Plume/smoke**: dense white-grey column with a bright orange-white core at the base; daytime shots (USS Vincennes 1987, image in images/) show a thick smoke trail hanging in the air after the missile has passed; night shots show mostly the flame core.
- **Disappears fast**: within roughly 5-6 seconds the missile is a shrinking dot/streak at the top of most ship-deck video frames.
- **Booster/case**: on VLS rounds using the separate Mk 72 first stage, the spent booster case separates and falls away after burnout, same as SM-6; no public footage documents the drop impact.
- **Cruise/climb profile**: continues boosting/coasting on a climbing intercept course rather than leveling into cruise flight — profile shape is a rising arc toward the target's predicted intercept point, not a cruise-missile low-level run.

## Manoeuvrability / drag notes
No public g-load or drag-curve figures for SM-2. Later blocks (Block IIIB, Block IV) added an IR seeker and improved terminal guidance but published material does not give maneuver-g figures. Speed decays after motor burnout as the missile coasts to intercept; no public deceleration curve.

## Where sources disagree
- Range is quoted anywhere from 40 nmi (early SM-2MR) to 90+ nmi (SM-2 Block IV, extended-range variants) — always check which block a figure is describing.
- Some sources describe SM-2 as single-stage (self-boosting) and others reference the two-stage Mk 72-boosted version used from VLS; both exist depending on variant/launcher.
