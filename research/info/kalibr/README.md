# 3M-54 Kalibr (3S14 VLS)

## Specs
- Length: 8.9 m (surface-launched 3M-54T variant). Diameter: 0.533 m. Mass: varies by variant, roughly
  1,300–2,300 kg depending on source/variant (3M-54 vs 3M-54T vs export 3M-54E).
  [Kalibr family — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalibr_(missile_family))
- Two-stage flight profile, subsonic cruise then supersonic terminal sprint — unusual among cruise missiles:
  - Cruise: ~Mach 0.8, altitude 50–150 m AGL over land, ~20 m over water. [Wikipedia]
  - Terminal: a separate rocket motor/stage kicks in for the last ~20 km (220 km range at supersonic speed is
    also cited, which looks like a units mix-up in some sources — treat "tens of km" terminal sprint as safer)
    accelerating to ~Mach 2.9, altitude drops to ~4.6 m (15 ft) — a hard, low, fast final dash.
- Launched from the 3S14 universal VLS (also fires Oniks/BrahMos from the same cell type), a cold-launch system
  (missile ejected from the cell, then ignites) — public sources don't give an exit-speed or ejection-height
  number specific to Kalibr's 3S14 cell.
  [3S14 background — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalibr_(missile_family))

## What a launch looks like
1. **Cell cover**: 3S14 VLS cells have a flush hatch/cover over each tube; it blows/flips open just before the
   missile emerges (same family behaviour as Western Mk 41 VLS — not confirmed frame-by-frame for 3S14).
2. **Cold launch**: missile is ejected from the cell by a gas charge before the main motor lights, consistent
   with all VLS-launched members of this family sharing the 3S14 cell (Oniks, Kalibr, BrahMos).
3. **Ignition in the air**: main rocket motor ignites once clear of the deck/cell — expect a brief silent-ish
   pop-up beat before the flame and roar start, then a fast vertical climb.
4. **Booster/cruise transition**: after the initial rocket boost the missile noses over onto its cruise heading
   and settles into low-altitude subsonic cruise (50–150 m land / ~20 m sea).
5. **Long cruise leg**: level, slow-ish (Mach 0.8), low flight — visually unremarkable compared to the ends of
   the flight; this is the bulk of the transit for long-range shots.
6. **Terminal sprint**: a second motor stage ignites, missile accelerates hard to supersonic and drops to
   sea-skim (~4.6 m) for the final approach — expect a new, more intense exhaust plume and a visible speed jump
   in the last seconds.
7. **Submarine/deck launches** (see `images/kalibr_launch_volkhov_submarine.jpg`, `klub_launch_ins_tabar.jpg`):
   vertical white rocket plume, a short soot/smoke cloud hanging at the launch point, missile visible as a
   bright point climbing out of the plume.

**Gaps**: no sourced booster burn time, VLS ejection speed, or precise pitch-over timing for the 3S14 cold
launch stage specifically (as opposed to the general Kalibr family description above).
